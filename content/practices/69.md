## 69. Theory of Constraints / Теорія обмежень

### EN

#### Q1 · single_choice · foundation · 1
In the Theory of Constraints, what is the constraint of a system?
A) The most expensive machine in the plant
B) The step that employs the most people
C) The step that limits how much the whole system can produce
D) The step that produces the most defects
**Answer:** C
**Why:** The constraint is whatever sets the pace of the whole system, so an hour lost there is an hour lost for the business. Cost, headcount and defect count may matter for other reasons, but they do not decide the output.

#### Q2 · single_choice · foundation · 1
Which sequence gives the five focusing steps in the right order?
A) Identify, exploit, subordinate, elevate, repeat
B) Elevate, identify, exploit, repeat, subordinate
C) Subordinate, elevate, identify, exploit, repeat
D) Measure, improve, control, standardise, repeat
**Answer:** A
**Why:** You first find the constraint, then squeeze the most from it with what you already have, align everything else to its pace, and only then spend money to add capacity, before going back to find the next constraint. The other orders spend money too early, and the last list belongs to another method.

#### Q3 · true_false · foundation · 1
An hour saved at a step that is not the bottleneck usually adds nothing to the output of the system.
**Answer:** true
**Why:** That step already has spare capacity, so the system keeps moving at the bottleneck’s pace and the saved hour turns into idle time.

#### Q4 · true_false · applied · 1
Once a bottleneck has been expanded, it stays at the same step, so there is no need to analyse the system again.
**Answer:** false
**Why:** Removing a constraint always reveals another one, which may be a different step, a policy, or market demand. That is why the five steps form a loop and the analysis must be repeated.

#### Q5 · single_choice · applied · 1
A print shop can design 120 jobs a day, print 80, bind 100 and pack 150. Customers want 90 jobs a day. Where should the owner add a night shift first?
A) Design
B) Binding
C) Packing
D) Printing
**Answer:** D
**Why:** Printing, at 80 jobs, caps the output below both the demand and every other step. A night shift in design, binding or packing would only pile up work or leave it waiting in front of the printers.

#### Q6 · multiple_select · applied · 2
A bakery’s oven is the bottleneck. Which **two** actions exploit it without buying anything?
A) Buying a second oven
B) Having staff cover the lunch break so the oven never stops
C) Loading it with trays that have already been checked for defects
D) Making the mixer run faster than the oven can bake
E) Releasing dough to the oven as soon as it is mixed, whatever its queue
**Answer:** B, C
**Why:** Keeping the oven running and feeding it only good work gets more output from the same capacity at no cost. A second oven is elevating the constraint, and pushing dough faster than the oven can use it only creates a queue.

#### Q7 · scenario_choice · applied · 1
A bakery can mix 500 kg of dough a day, bake 300 kg and pack 450 kg, while customers order only 280 kg. The owner wants to buy a larger mixer. What advice fits the theory?
A) Buy the mixer, because mixing is the first step in the line
B) Do not buy it: orders (280 kg) are below the oven’s capacity (300 kg), so the constraint is market demand and the effort belongs in sales
C) Buy another oven, because ovens are the most expensive equipment
D) Buy a packing line, because packing is the last step before the customer
**Answer:** B
**Why:** When demand is below the capacity of every step, no machine limits output, and extra capacity adds only cost. The real constraint is the market, so the useful investment is in selling more. The mixer already has spare capacity, and the other two options also add cost without raising output.

#### Q8 · scenario_choice · advanced · 2
A hospital’s MRI scanner is idle about 30% of the time, yet patients wait six weeks. The cause is that referrals need approval from one manager, who processes them in a batch on Fridays. What is the best action?
A) Buy a second scanner
B) Make the scans themselves faster
C) Change the approval policy: daily approvals and pre-approved referral rules for routine cases
D) Hire more radiologists to read the images
**Answer:** C
**Why:** The constraint here is a policy, not a machine: the scanner idles because work reaches it in weekly lumps. Changing the rule is cheaper and faster than adding equipment. A second scanner would sit idle too, and quicker scans or more readers would not change what limits the flow.

#### Q9 · multiple_select · advanced · 2
Which **two** statements describe drum-buffer-rope correctly?
A) The constraint sets the beat of the whole plant (the drum)
B) The buffer is a pile of stock kept in front of every machine
C) The rope is a signal that releases work only as fast as the constraint can use it
D) The rope is a bonus that pushes workers to speed up
E) The drum is the largest machine in the factory
**Answer:** A, C
**Why:** The constraint is the drum, a time buffer protects it from upstream disruption, and the rope feeds work in at its pace. The buffer sits before the constraint, not in front of every machine, and the drum and rope are not about machine size or pay.

#### Q10 · scenario_choice · advanced · 1
In a laundry, the ironer is the constraint, and each hour of ironing produces $300 of throughput (sales minus truly variable costs). A supervisor proposes an $8,000 upgrade that saves three hours a week at the polishing step, which is not the constraint. What is that upgrade worth in extra throughput?
A) About $900 a week
B) About $300 a week
C) About $2,400 a month
D) Close to zero, because the ironer still sets the output
**Answer:** D
**Why:** Polishing has spare capacity, so the three hours turn into idle time while the ironer keeps the same pace. The $300 value belongs to hours at the constraint, not to hours anywhere in the laundry.

#### Q11 · short_answer · applied · 4
A coffee roaster can roast 600 kg a day, grind 500 kg, pack 420 kg and ship 700 kg, while customers want 600 kg a day. Name the constraint, say how much demand goes unmet each day, and give one way to exploit the constraint without buying equipment.
**Sample:** Packing is the constraint at 420 kg, so only 420 kg a day goes out and 180 kg of demand is unmet. To exploit it, the roaster can keep the packing line running through breaks, remove changeovers by packing in bigger batches of one product, and send only pre-checked bags to it.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Identifies packing (420 kg) as the constraint and calculates the unmet demand (180 kg)
- 2 | Gives a concrete way to use the constraint better without buying equipment (covering breaks, fewer changeovers, defect-free inputs)

#### Q12 · short_answer · advanced · 4
Explain why keeping every machine busy can make a factory worse off. Use numbers.
**Sample:** Suppose the bottleneck packs 100 units a day while the machine before it makes 150. If both run flat out, 50 extra units pile up in front of the bottleneck every day: after 10 days, 500 unfinished units tie up cash, take space, hide quality problems and lengthen the lead time, yet output stays at 100. The better rule is to release work at the bottleneck’s pace, so the upstream machine slows down and its idle time costs nothing.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Explains the mechanism: work released faster than the constraint can absorb only builds inventory
- 1 | Uses concrete numbers correctly (for example 50 extra units a day, 500 after ten days)
- 1 | States the remedy: release work at the constraint’s pace (rope) and accept idle time elsewhere

#### Q13 · business_decision · advanced · 5
A software team’s monthly capacity by stage: design 12 features, coding 10, code review 6 (two senior reviewers), testing 9, release 15. The business wants 10 features a month. Choose one: (1) hire two more developers, (2) protect the reviewers’ time with automated checks before review and no meetings on review days, and limit the number of items waiting for review, or (3) hire another designer. Justify the choice.
**Sample:** Option 2. Review is the constraint at 6 features a month, so the whole team ships 6 however fast the others work. More developers or designers would only add to the queue in front of review. Automated checks and protected review time exploit the constraint at no cost, and a limit on waiting items subordinates the earlier stages to its pace. If that is not enough, elevate it, for example by training a third reviewer. Risk: the constraint will move to testing at 9, so check queues each month and move attention there.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Makes one clear choice
- 2 | Identifies review as the constraint with case numbers and shows why options 1 and 3 add no output
- 1 | Links the choice to exploiting and subordinating (and, if needed, elevating) the constraint
- 1 | Names the next likely constraint and how to monitor it

#### Q14 · business_decision · expert · 5
A factory’s constraint used to be the paint line at 70 units a day. After a $60,000 upgrade it makes 130. The other steps: assembly 100, packing 120. Orders are 90 units a day. Choose one: (1) buy a second assembly line for $80,000, (2) invest in sales, such as faster delivery promises and new dealers, keeping production as it is, or (3) upgrade the paint line again. Justify the choice.
**Sample:** Option 2. Assembly, at 100 units, now exceeds the 90 units ordered, so no step in the plant limits output and the constraint has moved to market demand. A second assembly line or more painting would add cost but not a single sold unit. Investing in sales raises throughput, and if orders rise past 100, assembly becomes the constraint and the next step is to exploit it with shift cover and fewer changeovers before buying the line. Risk: winning orders faster than expected may create late deliveries, so watch the assembly queue weekly.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Finds that market demand is now the constraint, using the case numbers (demand 90 below assembly 100)
- 1 | Makes one clear choice
- 1 | Explains why the other investments do not raise throughput
- 1 | Names the next constraint (assembly) and a way to watch it

#### Q15 · case_study · expert · 6
> An online store receives 1,500 orders a day. Picking handles 2,200 parcels a day, packing 1,800, labelling 1,300 (one machine) and shipping 2,000. The labeller stops for an hour at lunch and for five changeovers of 20 minutes between carriers, and the rule is to collect a batch of 500 parcels before moving them to labelling. The operations manager wants to hire ten more pickers because the picking area looks overloaded.
Identify the constraint, apply the first four focusing steps, explain why hiring pickers is a mistake, and name the measures you would track.
**Sample:** The constraint is labelling at 1,300 parcels a day, below demand of 1,500, so every day about 200 orders slip. Exploit: keep the labeller running through lunch with staff cover, group parcels by carrier to cut the changeovers, and give it only correctly packed parcels. Subordinate: pace picking and packing to the labeller, release orders at its speed, replace the batch of 500 with smaller batches and keep a small buffer in front of it. The picking area looks overloaded because work piles up there, so ten more pickers would raise cost and inventory but not shipped orders. Elevate only if exploiting is not enough, for example with a second labeller, then repeat the analysis, because the constraint will probably move to packing at 1,800. Track throughput (orders shipped), work in progress before labelling and operating expense.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Identifies labelling (1,300) as the constraint and compares it with demand (1,500)
- 1 | Gives concrete exploit actions (breaks, changeovers, defect-free input)
- 1 | Subordinates the other steps and explains why the new pickers add cost, not output
- 2 | Plans elevate and repeat, and names measures (throughput, inventory, operating expense)

Що в теорії обмежень є обмеженням системи?

#### Q13 · business_decision · advanced · 5

Обмеженням заводу була лінія фарбування з 70 одиницями на день. Після оновлення за $60 000 вона робить 130. Інші етапи: складання 100, пакування 120. Замовлень 90 одиниць на день. Оберіть одне: (1) купити другу лінію складання за $80 000, (2) вкласти в продажі, як-от швидші обіцянки щодо доставки й нові дилери, лишивши виробництво без змін, або (3) знову оновити лінію фарбування. Обґрунтуйте вибір.
**Sample:** Варіант 2. Складання зі 100 одиницями тепер перевищує 90 замовлених одиниць, тож жоден етап заводу не обмежує випуск, а обмеження перемістилося до ринкового попиту. #### Q15 · case_study · expert · 6
> Інтернет-магазин отримує 1 500 замовлень на день. Комплектування обробляє 2 200 посилок на день, пакування 1 800, маркування 1 300 (одна машина), а відправлення 2 000. Маркувальна машина зупиняється на годину на обід і на п’ять переналагоджень по 20 хвилин між перевізниками, а за правилом перед маркуванням збирають партію з 500 посилок. Керівник операцій хоче найняти десять комплектувальників, бо зона комплектування виглядає перевантаженою.
Визначте обмеження, застосуйте перші чотири кроки фокусування, поясніть, чому найм комплектувальників помилка, і назвіть показники, за якими стежили б.
**Sample:** Обмеження це маркування з 1 300 посилок на день, що менше за попит 1 500, тож щодня близько 200 замовлень зсувається. Максимально використати: тримати маркувальну машину в роботі під час обіду з підміною персоналу, групувати посилки за перевізником, щоб скоротити переналагодження, і подавати на неї лише правильно запаковані посилки. Підпорядкувати: узгодити темп комплектування й пакування з маркуванням, випускати замовлення в його швидкості, замінити партію з 500 меншими партіями й тримати невеликий буфер перед нею. Зона комплектування виглядає перевантаженою, бо там накопичується робота, тож десять нових комплектувальників збільшили б витрати й запаси, але не кількість відправлених замовлень. Розширювати лише тоді, коли максимального використання не вистачає, наприклад другою маркувальною машиною, а далі повторити аналіз, бо обмеження, найімовірніше, перейде на пакування з 1 800. Стежити за пропускною здатністю (відправлені замовлення), незавершеною роботою перед маркуванням і операційними витратами.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Визначає маркування (1 300) як обмеження й порівнює його з попитом (1 500)
- 1 | Дає конкретні дії для максимального використання (перерви, переналагодження, вхід без браку)
- 1 | Підпорядковує інші етапи й пояснює, чому нові комплектувальники додають витрат, а не випуску
- 2 | Планує розширення та повторення й називає показники (пропускна здатність, запаси, операційні витрати)
