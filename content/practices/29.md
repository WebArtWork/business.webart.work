## 29. Hype Cycle / Цикл ажіотажу

### EN

#### Q1 · single_choice · foundation · 1
Which phase of the Hype Cycle comes straight after the peak of inflated expectations?
A) Trough of disillusionment
B) Slope of enlightenment
C) Plateau of productivity
D) Innovation trigger
**Answer:** A
**Why:** After the peak, real deployments reveal costs and limits, and sentiment crashes into the trough. The slope and plateau come later, and the trigger is the very first phase.

#### Q2 · single_choice · foundation · 1
Second-generation products appear, vendors publish integration guides, and the realistic benefits become clearer. Which phase is this?
A) Peak of inflated expectations
B) Trough of disillusionment
C) Slope of enlightenment
D) Innovation trigger
**Answer:** C
**Why:** These are the marks of the slope of enlightenment, when practical knowledge catches up. The peak is driven by publicity, the trough by failed experiments and exiting vendors, and the trigger comes before products exist.

#### Q3 · true_false · foundation · 1
The Hype Cycle is a reliable timetable: every technology reaches the plateau within the number of years Gartner estimates.
**Answer:** false
**Why:** Technologies move through the curve at very different speeds, some skip phases and some never reach the plateau. Positions and estimates are expert judgments, not a schedule.

#### Q4 · true_false · applied · 1
The number of articles and conference talks about a technology is a weaker signal of its position on the curve than the number of paying production deployments.
**Answer:** true
**Why:** Media buzz measures visibility, not maturity. The position should be judged from evidence of real use, such as paying customers and production deployments.

#### Q5 · single_choice · applied · 1
A restaurant chain with 60 outlets looks at AI voice ordering. Media coverage is everywhere, vendors promise 30% labour savings in six months, two rivals have announced pilots, and few production deployments exist. What is the best action?
A) Roll it out across all 60 restaurants now to stay ahead
B) Watch closely and at most run a very small test, since promises at the peak rarely survive real integration
C) Drop the idea for good, because hype means it will fail
D) Sign a five-year exclusive contract at today's prices
**Answer:** B
**Why:** The signals point to the peak, where the right move is to watch or test cheaply. A full rollout or long contract locks in peak prices and promises, and dropping it ignores that many technologies reach the plateau later.

#### Q6 · multiple_select · applied · 2
Which **two** signals suggest that a technology is in the trough of disillusionment?
A) Vendors raise prices and hire aggressively.
B) Major newspapers run cover stories promising a revolution.
C) Several vendors have merged or closed, and early adopters publish failure stories.
D) A first lab demo is shown at a trade fair.
E) Interest fades after many pilots failed to deliver the promised savings.
**Answer:** C, E
**Why:** Vendor exits, failure stories and fading interest after failed experiments define the trough. Rising prices and cover stories belong to the peak, and a first demo is the innovation trigger.

#### Q7 · scenario_choice · applied · 1
A logistics firm piloted blockchain shipment tracking at the peak of the hype. The pilot failed because partners' systems could not connect to it. The technology now sits in the trough. What should the firm ask first?
A) Should we declare the technology useless and close the file?
B) When will the media buzz return?
C) What is our biggest rival doing with it?
D) Did the failure come from the tool itself or from immature integration that is now improving?
**Answer:** D
**Why:** Abandoning a technology in the trough is a common mistake, so the key question is whether the cause is fixable. Media buzz is not evidence, and copying a rival skips the analysis.

#### Q8 · scenario_choice · advanced · 2
A hospital group's analyst places "AI in healthcare" on the plateau of productivity because it is in the news every day. What is the best correction?
A) Name the technology narrowly, such as "AI reading chest X-rays for triage", and place it by evidence of use: paying reference sites, deployments and pilot results
B) Accept the placement, since that much media coverage shows maturity
C) Place it at the peak by default, since all AI is hyped
D) Skip the placement and buy from the cheapest vendor
**Answer:** A
**Why:** A broad label mixes technologies at different phases, and media volume measures visibility, not maturity. Placing it at the peak by default is just as unsupported, and skipping the analysis loses the timing guidance.

#### Q9 · multiple_select · advanced · 2
A retailer's innovation team reviewed smart shelves. It (1) wrote "smart shelves reach the plateau in 2027, so budget the rollout for 2027", (2) collected pilot results and failure stories from other retailers, (3) planned to recheck the position every six to twelve months, (4) dropped the idea after one disappointing pilot without checking the cause, and (5) ran a small pilot in one store. Which **two** were mistakes?
A) Treating the curve as a timetable in step 1
B) Collecting pilot results and failure stories in step 2
C) Planning regular rechecks in step 3
D) Dropping the idea after one pilot without checking the cause in step 4
E) Running a small pilot in one store in step 5
**Answer:** A, D
**Why:** Budgeting by a predicted plateau date treats the curve as a schedule, and quitting without asking whether the tool or the integration failed is the trough mistake. Gathering evidence, rechecking and small pilots are what the method recommends.

#### Q10 · scenario_choice · advanced · 1
A CFO asks whether the Hype Cycle can tell her if a technology on the plateau suits her company. What is the right answer?
A) Yes: once on the plateau, every technology pays back.
B) No: the curve tracks expectations, not economics, so she still needs a business case for her company.
C) Yes, as long as the technology passed quickly through the trough.
D) Only if Gartner has published a report on that technology.
**Answer:** B
**Why:** The model focuses on expectations and maturity and says nothing about fit with a particular business. A mature technology can still be wrong for her costs and processes, and speed through the trough or a Gartner report does not change that.

#### Q11 · short_answer · applied · 4
A farming cooperative is considering crop-spraying drones. Two years ago the buzz was huge and vendors promised 40% savings on chemicals. Last year 3 of 7 vendors left the market and farmers reported spray drift problems. Now second-generation drones with drift control are available, 120 farms in the region use them in production, and realistic savings are about 15%. Place the technology on the curve and say what the cooperative should do.
**Sample:** It is on the slope of enlightenment. The peak was two years ago (huge buzz, 40% promises), the trough came last year (vendor exits, drift problems), and now second-generation products, 120 farms in production and a realistic 15% saving show practical knowledge catching up. On the slope, the right move is to scale carefully: start with a season on a few member farms, measure the real saving against 15%, then extend to more farms if it holds.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Places it on the slope of enlightenment and cites evidence of the earlier peak and trough plus current production use
- 2 | Recommends an action that fits the slope (pilot, then scale step by step) with a measure to check

#### Q12 · short_answer · advanced · 4
Explain why expectations overshoot at the peak and then overcorrect in the trough. Use virtual reality for employee training as the example.
**Sample:** Two forces overlap: excitement from early stories spreads fast, while practical knowledge about costs and integration builds slowly. Early VR training demos showed staff learning twice as fast, and these stories spread before anyone knew the costs of headsets, content and motion sickness. So expectations peaked too high. When companies ran real programmes and met those costs, disappointment swung sentiment below the true value. As experience grows and better content tools appear, expectations rise again to match real results.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Names the two forces: fast-spreading excitement and slow-building practical knowledge
- 1 | Explains the overcorrection: real deployments reveal costs, so sentiment falls below true value
- 1 | Applies both to VR training with concrete details

#### Q13 · business_decision · advanced · 5
A bank is reviewing customer-service chatbots. At the peak, vendors promised a 50% cut in calls. Since then two vendors have exited, prices have fallen 60%, survivors publish integration guides, and real pilots show 15–20% fewer calls on simple questions. Options: a full rollout that replaces 40% of call staff, a $60,000 pilot on balance and card questions, or waiting three years. Choose one and justify it with the curve.
**Sample:** The $60,000 pilot. The signals put chatbots in the trough moving toward the slope: vendor exits, falling prices and published integration guides. The trough is the best time for a careful buyer to pilot cheaply with clearer evidence. A full rollout planned on cutting 40% of staff assumes savings twice the 15–20% that pilots actually show. Waiting three years gives up the low prices and lets rivals learn first. I would measure the share of balance and card questions resolved without an agent and expand only if it reaches about 15%.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Makes one clear choice
- 2 | Places the technology in the trough (or early slope) with evidence and explains why that suits a cheap pilot
- 1 | Shows why the full rollout relies on peak promises, using the numbers
- 1 | Sets a measurable condition for expanding

#### Q14 · business_decision · expert · 5
A furniture manufacturer has $400,000 for one technology project.
> Generative design software: in the news everywhere, vendors promise 50% faster design, two reference customers.
> Camera-based quality inspection: second-generation products, 40 production deployments in furniture, realistic defect reduction of 30%. The manufacturer's defects cost $900,000 a year.
Choose one project and justify it with the Hype Cycle, noting what the curve cannot tell you.
**Sample:** Camera-based inspection. Its signals (second-generation products, 40 production deployments, realistic figures) place it on the slope, where scaling makes sense. Cutting defects by 30% would save about $270,000 a year, so $400,000 pays back in about a year and a half. Generative design sits at the peak: heavy publicity, big promises and only two references, so I would only watch it or run a tiny trial. The curve does not prove the inspection suits our lines; the payback estimate, not the curve, makes that case. I would recheck generative design in six to twelve months.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Makes one clear choice
- 1 | Places both technologies on the curve with evidence from the case
- 1 | Uses the numbers (30% of $900,000, payback on $400,000)
- 1 | Matches the action to each phase (scale on the slope, watch at the peak)
- 1 | Notes a limit of the curve (expectations, not economics, or judgment-based positions) or plans a recheck

#### Q15 · case_study · expert · 6
> A dental lab makes 4,000 crowns a year by milling, at a cost of $90 each. Four years ago, 3D-printed crowns were the talk of every trade show, and vendors promised a cost of $20 a crown.
> Two years ago, two labs bought $150,000 printers; one reported that 30% of printed crowns had to be remade, and 2 of 5 printer vendors left the market.
> Today, second-generation certified resins are available, 18% of labs in the region print crowns in production, the realistic cost is $45 a crown with a 6% remake rate, and printers cost $60,000.
Place 3D-printed crowns on the Hype Cycle, describe the path that led there, and recommend what the lab should do.
**Sample:** Four years ago was the peak ($20 promises, trade-show buzz), two years ago the trough (30% remakes, vendors leaving), and today the slope of enlightenment: certified second-generation resins, 18% of labs in production and realistic costs. At $45 instead of $90, printing all 4,000 crowns would save about $180,000 a year, so a $60,000 printer pays back in months even if only part of the volume moves. Action: buy one printer, print a share of simple crowns for three months and track the remake rate against 6%, then move more volume if it holds. Recheck the market in a year.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Places the technology on the slope and traces the earlier peak and trough with evidence
- 1 | Calculates the saving correctly (4,000 × $45 = $180,000 a year) and relates it to the printer cost
- 2 | Recommends an action fitted to the slope: a pilot with a measurable check, then scaling
- 1 | Plans a recheck or notes that the position is a judgment that can be wrong

#### Q1 · single_choice · foundation · 1
Яка фаза циклу ажіотажу настає одразу після піку завищених очікувань?
A) Западина розчарування
B) Схил просвітлення

**Answer:** A
**Why:** Після піку реальні впровадження показують витрати й обмеження, і настрої обвалюються у западину. Схил і плато настають пізніше, а поштовх це найперша фаза.

#### Q2 · single_choice · foundation · 1
З’являються продукти другого покоління, постачальники публікують посібники з інтеграції, а реальні переваги стають зрозумілішими. Що це за фаза?
A) Пік завищених очікувань
B) Западина розчарування
C) Схил просвітлення
D) Технологічний поштовх
**Answer:** C
**Why:** Це ознаки схилу просвітлення, коли практичні знання наздоганяють очікування. Пік живиться розголосом, западина позначена провальними експериментами й відходом постачальників, а поштовх стається ще до появи продуктів.

#### Q3 · true_false · foundation · 1
Цикл ажіотажу це надійний розклад: кожна технологія досягає плато за ту кількість років, яку оцінює Gartner.
**Answer:** false
**Why:** Технології проходять криву з дуже різною швидкістю, деякі пропускають фази, а деякі так і не досягають плато. Позиції й оцінки це експертні судження, а не графік.

#### Q4 · true_false · applied · 1
Кількість статей і виступів на конференціях про технологію є слабшим сигналом її позиції на кривій, ніж кількість платних промислових впроваджень.
**Answer:** true
**Why:** Галас у медіа вимірює помітність, а не зрілість. Позицію треба оцінювати за доказами реального використання, як-от клієнтами, що платять, і промисловими впровадженнями.

#### Q5 · single_choice · applied · 1

B) Уважно стежити й щонайбільше провести дуже малий тест, бо обіцянки на піку рідко витримують реальну інтеграцію
