## 64. Cash Flow Cycle / Цикл руху грошових коштів

### EN

#### Q1 · single_choice · foundation · 1
How is the cash conversion cycle calculated?
A) DIO + DSO + DPO
B) DSO − DIO + DPO
C) DPO − DIO − DSO
D) DIO + DSO − DPO
**Answer:** D
**Why:** Stock waits (DIO), then invoices wait (DSO), and supplier credit covers part of that time, so DPO is subtracted. Adding DPO counts supplier credit as extra waiting, and the other two reverse the logic.

#### Q2 · single_choice · foundation · 1
What does days sales outstanding (DSO) measure?
A) How long customers take to pay
B) How long stock sits before it is sold
C) How long the business takes to pay its suppliers
D) How long an order takes to be delivered
**Answer:** A
**Why:** DSO is receivables divided by sales per day, the wait for customer cash. Stock time is DIO, supplier payment time is DPO, and delivery time is not part of the cycle formula.

#### Q3 · true_false · foundation · 1
A business that reports a profit every month cannot run out of cash.
**Answer:** false
**Why:** Profit is recorded when a sale is made, but cash returns only after stock is sold and invoices are paid. A growing firm can be profitable and still empty its bank account because each new sale ties up more money.

#### Q4 · true_false · applied · 1
A web design agency that holds no inventory can still lock up a lot of cash in its cash conversion cycle.
**Answer:** true
**Why:** With no stock, DIO is close to zero, but if clients pay 60 days after invoicing while staff are paid monthly, DSO alone creates a long gap that the agency must finance.

#### Q5 · single_choice · applied · 1
A hardware distributor has DIO of 50 days, DSO of 40 days and DPO of 35 days. Its cost of goods sold is $6,000 a day. How much cash does the cycle lock up?
A) $750,000
B) $330,000
C) $540,000
D) $210,000
**Answer:** B
**Why:** The cycle is 50 + 40 − 35 = 55 days, and 55 × $6,000 = $330,000. $750,000 adds DPO instead of subtracting it, $540,000 ignores supplier credit, and $210,000 uses only the DPO days.

#### Q6 · multiple_select · applied · 2
A pet-food wholesaler wants to shorten its cycle without hurting the business. Which **two** actions fit?
A) Drop slow-moving products that sit in the warehouse for 120 days
B) Send invoices on the day of delivery instead of at month end
C) Pay suppliers 30 days late without telling them
D) Cut stock of best-sellers so they run out every week
E) Offer customers 60-day terms to win more orders
**Answer:** A, B
**Why:** Clearing slow stock lowers DIO, and faster invoicing lowers DSO, with no damage to sales or suppliers. Paying late without agreement destroys trust and discounts, running out of best-sellers loses more than it saves, and 60-day terms lengthen the cycle.

#### Q7 · scenario_choice · applied · 1
An online cosmetics shop sells its stock in 25 days, is paid by card within 2 days and pays suppliers in 45 days. The owner asks whether faster growth will need a loan for working capital. What is the best answer?
A) Yes, because growth always consumes cash
B) Yes, about 72 days of costs must be financed
C) No: the cycle is about −18 days, so suppliers fund the operations and growth generates cash
D) It cannot be judged without the profit figure
**Answer:** C
**Why:** 25 + 2 − 45 = −18 days, so cash comes in before suppliers must be paid, and more sales release more cash. Growth consumes cash only when the cycle is positive, 72 days wrongly adds DPO, and the cycle is judged without profit data.

#### Q8 · scenario_choice · advanced · 2
A machine-parts maker grew sales by 30% and reports record profit, yet its bank account is empty. Its DIO is 60 days, DSO 75 days and DPO 30 days. Daily cost of goods rose from $10,000 to $13,000. What is the best diagnosis and response?
A) The 105-day cycle now locks up about $315,000 more; shorten DSO with deposits, milestone billing or early-payment discounts before growing further
B) Cut prices so stock sells faster and the cash returns
C) Stop paying suppliers until the cash recovers
D) The accounts must be wrong, since profit and cash should match
**Answer:** A
**Why:** The cycle is 60 + 75 − 30 = 105 days, and $3,000 more per day × 105 = $315,000 extra cash tied up. DSO is the biggest part, so collecting sooner is the strongest lever. Price cuts hurt margins, stopping payments can cut off supply, and profit and cash routinely differ.

#### Q9 · multiple_select · advanced · 2
A toy retailer calculated its cycle from 31 December balances only. Stock was very low after the holiday rush, giving DIO of 20 days, and receivables include $40,000 owed by a chain that has gone bankrupt. Which **two** limitations of the measure apply?
A) Year-end balances mislead in a seasonal business, so yearly averages are more honest.
B) The ratio does not show quality: some receivables may never be collected.
C) The formula should add DPO rather than subtract it.
D) Retailers cannot use the cash conversion cycle.
E) The cycle must be measured in months, not days.
**Answer:** A, B
**Why:** December stock is unusually low for a toy seller, and bad debts make DSO look like money that will arrive. DPO is correctly subtracted, retailers use the measure widely, and days are the standard unit.

#### Q10 · scenario_choice · advanced · 1
A restaurant supplier wants to free cash by stretching its DPO from 30 to 90 days, simply by paying later. Its main suppliers offer a 2% discount for paying within 10 days. What is the best course?
A) Pay at 90 days, because supplier money is free money
B) Pay at 90 days only to the smallest suppliers, who cannot push back
C) Never use supplier credit at all
D) Negotiate longer terms openly where it costs nothing, and keep taking the early-payment discounts that are worth more than the cash
**Answer:** D
**Why:** Paying late without agreement loses discounts, damages trust and can raise prices or cut off supply. Squeezing small suppliers just shifts the strain to them, and refusing all supplier credit gives up a normal source of financing.

#### Q11 · short_answer · applied · 4
An electronics repair chain reports for last year: average inventory $90,000, cost of goods sold $730,000, average receivables $40,000, sales $1,095,000, average payables $50,000. Calculate DIO, DSO, DPO, the cycle and the cash it locks up.
**Sample:** Cost per day = $730,000 / 365 = $2,000; sales per day = $1,095,000 / 365 = $3,000. DIO = $90,000 / $2,000 = 45 days. DSO = $40,000 / $3,000 ≈ 13 days. DPO = $50,000 / $2,000 = 25 days. Cycle = 45 + 13 − 25 ≈ 33 days, so about 33 × $2,000 ≈ $66,700 is locked up. Inventory is the largest part, so that is where to look first.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Calculates DIO of 45, DSO of about 13 and DPO of 25 days
- 1 | Combines them into a cycle of about 33 days
- 1 | Converts the cycle into cash locked up (about $66,000–67,000)

#### Q12 · short_answer · advanced · 4
Explain why a fast-growing distributor can be profitable and still run out of cash. Use a small numeric example.
**Sample:** Cash tied up equals daily cost times the cycle in days. A distributor with a 60-day cycle and $5,000 of daily cost has $300,000 locked in stock and receivables. If sales double while the days stay the same, it needs $600,000, so $300,000 of extra cash leaves before the new profits arrive. The income statement shows profit on every sale, but the bank account pays for the growing pipeline first.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | States the mechanism: cash tied up = daily volume × cycle days, and it grows with sales
- 1 | Gives a correct numeric example of the extra cash required
- 1 | Explains the gap between profit recorded on sales and cash returning later

#### Q13 · business_decision · advanced · 5
A handmade-furniture online shop needs $120,000 to open a second workshop. Its daily cost of goods is $3,000. It has three ways to find the money:
> Option 1: a bank loan of $120,000 at 12% a year.
> Option 2: a working-capital programme: clear slow models to cut DIO from 80 to 60 days, and take 30% deposits to cut DSO from 40 to 20 days.
> Option 3: pay all suppliers 40 days later than agreed, without negotiating.
Choose one option and justify it with numbers and risks.
**Sample:** Option 2. Cutting DIO by 20 days and DSO by 20 days frees 40 × $3,000 = $120,000, exactly the amount needed, with no interest, while the loan costs $14,400 a year. Option 3 frees the same $120,000 on paper, but unilateral late payment loses trust and discounts and can stop supply. Risk of Option 2: corporate buyers may refuse deposits, so offer them a small discount for paying upfront and track DSO monthly; if the programme falls short, borrow only the gap.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Makes one clear choice
- 2 | Calculates the cash freed by the programme ($120,000) and the loan's yearly cost ($14,400)
- 1 | Explains why unilateral late payment is harmful despite freeing the same cash
- 1 | Names a risk of the choice and a way to manage it

#### Q14 · business_decision · expert · 5
An industrial bakery supplies cafés. Its daily cost of goods is $5,000, with DIO 10 days, DSO 20 days and DPO 25 days. A supermarket chain offers a contract that would add $5,000 a day of cost and $600 a day of profit, but it pays in 90 days. The bakery has $60,000 of spare cash and can borrow at 10%. Accept as offered, reject, or accept with conditions? Decide and show the numbers.
**Sample:** Accept with conditions. For the new volume the cycle is 10 + 90 − 25 = 75 days, so it locks up 75 × $5,000 = $375,000, far beyond the $60,000 available. The contract earns about $600 × 365 = $219,000 a year, and financing $375,000 at 10% costs $37,500, so it is worth having but cannot be funded as offered. Conditions: negotiate 60-day terms, which cuts the extra cash to 45 × $5,000 = $225,000, ramp up volume in phases and sign a credit line or invoice factoring before the first delivery.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Makes one clear choice
- 2 | Calculates the new-volume cycle (75 days) and the extra cash locked up (about $375,000)
- 1 | Weighs the contract's profit against the financing cost
- 1 | Sets concrete conditions (shorter terms, phased ramp-up, financing in place) that close the cash gap

#### Q15 · case_study · expert · 6
> A clothing brand reports for last year: cost of goods sold $1,460,000, average inventory $480,000, sales $2,190,000, average receivables $270,000, average payables $120,000.
> Similar brands run at DIO 75 days, DSO 30 days and DPO 45 days.
> The founder plans 25% sales growth next year and wants to know how much cash it will need.
Calculate the brand's cycle and the cash it locks up, find the weakest part, estimate the cash freed by reaching peer levels and the extra cash growth would need at today's cycle, and propose levers.
**Sample:** Cost per day $4,000, sales per day $6,000. DIO = 480,000 / 4,000 = 120; DSO = 270,000 / 6,000 = 45; DPO = 120,000 / 4,000 = 30. Cycle 135 days, locking up 135 × $4,000 = $540,000. Inventory is the weakest part, 45 days above peers. The peer cycle of 60 days would need $240,000, freeing $300,000. At today's cycle, 25% growth adds 135 × $1,000 = $135,000. Levers: cut slow styles and smaller first orders, invoice on shipment, and agree 45-day terms with key factories openly.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Calculates DIO 120, DSO 45, DPO 30, a 135-day cycle and $540,000 locked up
- 1 | Identifies inventory as the weakest part compared with peers
- 1 | Estimates about $300,000 freed at the peer cycle
- 1 | Estimates about $135,000 of extra cash for 25% growth at today's cycle
- 1 | Proposes levers that do not harm sales or supplier trust

#### Q1 · single_choice · foundation · 1
Як розраховують цикл перетворення грошових коштів?
A) DIO + DSO + DPO
B) DSO − DIO + DPO
C) DPO − DIO − DSO
D) DIO + DSO − DPO
**Answer:** D
**Why:** Спершу чекають запаси (DIO), потім рахунки клієнтів (DSO), а кредит постачальників покриває частину цього часу, тому DPO віднімають. Додавання DPO рахує кредит постачальників як додаткове очікування, а два інші варіанти перевертають логіку.

#### Q2 · single_choice · foundation · 1
Що вимірює період обороту дебіторської заборгованості (DSO)?
A) Скільки часу клієнти платять
B) Скільки часу запаси лежать до продажу
C) Скільки часу бізнес розраховується з постачальниками
D) Скільки часу триває доставка замовлення
**Answer:** A
**Why:** DSO це дебіторська заборгованість, поділена на продажі за день, тобто очікування грошей від клієнтів. Час запасів це DIO, оплата постачальникам це DPO, а час доставки не входить у формулу циклу.

#### Q3 · true_false · foundation · 1
Бізнес, який щомісяця показує прибуток, не може залишитися без грошей.
**Answer:** false
**Why:** Прибуток фіксують у момент продажу, а гроші повертаються лише після продажу запасів і оплати рахунків. Компанія, що зростає, може бути прибутковою й водночас спорожнити рахунок, бо кожен новий продаж заморожує більше грошей.

#### Q4 · true_false · applied · 1
Вебстудія без жодних запасів усе одно може заморожувати багато грошей у циклі перетворення грошових коштів.
**Answer:** true
**Why:** Без запасів DIO майже нульовий, але якщо клієнти платять через 60 днів після рахунку, а зарплату платять щомісяця, один лише DSO створює довгий розрив, який студія мусить фінансувати.

#### Q5 · single_choice · applied · 1
Дистриб’ютор будівельних товарів має DIO 50 днів, DSO 40 днів і DPO 35 днів. Його собівартість продажів становить $6 000 на день. Скільки грошей заморожує цикл?
A) $750 000
B) $330 000
C) $540 000
D) $210 000
**Answer:** B
**Why:** Цикл становить 50 + 40 − 35 = 55 днів, а 55 × $6 000 = $330 000. $750 000 додає DPO замість віднімати, $540 000 ігнорує кредит постачальників, а $210 000 бере лише дні DPO.

#### Q6 · multiple_select · applied · 2

#### Q15 · case_study · expert · 6
> Бренд одягу звітує за минулий рік: собівартість продажів $1 460 000, середні запаси $480 000, продажі $2 190 000, середня дебіторська заборгованість $270 000, середня кредиторська заборгованість $120 000.
> Схожі бренди працюють із DIO 75 днів, DSO 30 днів і DPO 45 днів.
> Засновниця планує наступного року зростання продажів на 25% і хоче знати, скільки грошей для цього знадобиться.
Розрахуйте цикл бренду й заморожені в ньому гроші, знайдіть найслабшу ланку, оцініть, скільки грошей вивільнить вихід на рівень конкурентів і скільки додатково потребуватиме зростання за нинішнього циклу, і запропонуйте важелі.
**Sample:** Собівартість за день $4 000, продажі за день $6 000. DIO = 480 000 / 4 000 = 120; DSO = 270 000 / 6 000 = 45; DPO = 120 000 / 4 000 = 30. Цикл 135 днів, заморожено 135 × $4 000 = $540 000. Найслабша ланка це запаси, на 45 днів гірше за конкурентів. Цикл конкурентів у 60 днів потребував би $240 000, тобто вивільнив би $300 000. За нинішнього циклу зростання на 25% додасть 135 × $1 000 = $135 000. Важелі: скоротити повільні моделі й менші перші партії, виставляти рахунки при відвантаженні, відкрито домовитися про 45 днів із ключовими фабриками.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Рахує DIO 120, DSO 45, DPO 30, цикл 135 днів і $540 000 заморожених грошей
- 1 | Визначає запаси як найслабшу ланку порівняно з конкурентами
- 1 | Оцінює близько $300 000 вивільнених грошей за циклу конкурентів
- 1 | Оцінює близько $135 000 додаткових грошей на зростання 25% за нинішнього циклу
- 1 | Пропонує важелі, що не шкодять продажам і довірі постачальників
