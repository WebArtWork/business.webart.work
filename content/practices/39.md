## 39. Aggregation Theory / Теорія агрегаторів

### EN

#### Q1 · single_choice · foundation · 1
Before the internet, a city newspaper earned high margins from classified ads. According to Aggregation Theory, what resource has become scarce instead now that distribution is nearly free?
A) Printing capacity
B) User attention
C) Physical shelf space
D) Supplier inventory
**Answer:** B
**Why:** When distribution and transaction costs fall to almost zero, the scarce thing is users’ attention, and whoever owns the relationship with users gains power. Printing capacity and shelf space were the old scarce distribution, and supplier inventory becomes interchangeable once an aggregator controls demand.

#### Q2 · single_choice · foundation · 1
Which of these businesses fits the definition of an aggregator best?
A) A taxi firm that owns 300 cars and employs every driver
B) A furniture retailer that sells through its own stores
C) A staffing agency that recruits each nurse in person and pays signing bonuses
D) A recipe app with 2 million users where food bloggers post recipes for free to reach them, at about $0.01 per extra user
**Answer:** D
**Why:** The recipe app owns the user relationship, serves extra users almost free and pulls suppliers in because the audience is there. The taxi firm and the retailer own their supply, and the agency must recruit suppliers one by one at real cost.

#### Q3 · true_false · foundation · 1
An aggregator can dominate a market without owning the goods or services that change hands on it.
**Answer:** true
**Why:** Aggregators control demand, not supply. Suppliers join because the users are there, so the aggregator holds the power without owning hotels, cars or apartments.

#### Q4 · true_false · applied · 1
Once an aggregator has a large user base, users are locked in, so it no longer needs to keep improving the user experience.
**Answer:** false
**Why:** Users of an aggregator face little lock-in on the demand side, so a rival with a better experience can draw them away. Treating aggregator power as permanent is a common mistake.

#### Q5 · single_choice · applied · 1
A boutique hotel gets 65% of its bookings through a booking platform that takes an 18% commission. What does Aggregation Theory suggest the hotel should do as a supplier?
A) Leave the platform tomorrow and rely on its own website
B) Sign an exclusive deal with the platform for better ranking
C) Measure its dependence and build direct channels such as a guest email list and perks for booking direct
D) Lower room prices on the platform to rank higher than nearby hotels
**Answer:** C
**Why:** The theory advises suppliers to measure the share of demand coming through an aggregator and to build direct channels. Leaving at once throws away 65% of bookings, exclusivity deepens dependence, and price cuts on the platform only make the hotel a cheaper interchangeable option.

#### Q6 · multiple_select · applied · 2
A founder calls her B2B marketplace “an aggregator”. Which **two** facts suggest it does **not** have aggregator dynamics?
A) Every new buyer needs a sales visit that costs about $400
B) Suppliers now join unpaid because buyers are already there
C) After three years, the platform still pays a bonus to every new supplier it signs
D) 70% of buyers open the platform directly rather than searching the web
E) Serving one more buyer costs about $0.02 in hosting
**Answer:** A, C
**Why:** A high cost to serve each buyer and suppliers who must be paid one by one forever are the two signs that the dynamics are different. Unpaid supplier sign-ups, direct visits and near-zero serving cost are what an aggregator looks like.

#### Q7 · scenario_choice · applied · 1
A startup lists independent music teachers. It has 30 teachers and 200 students, and $100,000 to spend next. Where should it put the money if it wants to become an aggregator?
A) Into the student experience: easy search, honest reviews and one-tap trial lessons, letting teachers follow the demand
B) Into exclusive contracts with 300 teachers at $200 each
C) Into a building where all lessons take place
D) Into hiring salaried teachers so quality is controlled
**Answer:** A
**Why:** Aggregators win by delighting users first, and supply follows the demand. Paying for exclusive supply starts from the wrong side, while a building or salaried staff add the physical costs that break the zero-marginal-cost model.

#### Q8 · scenario_choice · advanced · 2
A food delivery app raises its restaurant commission from 25% to 30%. A restaurant group with eight locations gets 55% of its orders through the app. What is the best move for the group?
A) Leave the app immediately in protest
B) Accept the rise, since the app’s power over demand is permanent
C) Stay on the app for discovery, list on rival apps too and steer repeat customers to its own ordering channel with loyalty rewards
D) Agree to work only with this app in exchange for going back to 25%
**Answer:** C
**Why:** Suppliers weaken an aggregator’s hold by multi-homing and building direct channels, while still using it to reach new users. Leaving at once loses 55% of orders, exclusivity deepens dependence, and the theory warns that aggregator power is not permanent.

#### Q9 · multiple_select · advanced · 2
A ride-hailing app holds 80% of trips in a city. Which **two** developments would weaken its hold, according to the theory’s limitations?
A) Drivers organise to run two rival apps at the same time
B) More riders join, so more drivers sign up
C) The competition regulator opens an investigation into its commission rules
D) Its server cost per ride request falls further
E) It adds new car categories to the app
**Answer:** A, C
**Why:** Suppliers multi-homing and antitrust action are both named as ways the aggregator’s hold weakens. More riders, cheaper servers and more choice strengthen the flywheel instead.

#### Q10 · scenario_choice · advanced · 1
A travel search site handles 70% of local holiday searches but has lost $20 million a year for three years. An analyst says this proves Aggregation Theory wrong. Which reply is correct?
A) The theory predicts who controls demand, not that the aggregator will be profitable, and many aggregators lose money for years
B) The site cannot be an aggregator, because aggregators are always profitable
C) The losses prove that the hotels, not the site, hold the power
D) The theory only applies to physical goods, so it says nothing about travel search
**Answer:** A
**Why:** One limitation of the theory is that it predicts power but not profits. Losses do not show who holds power over demand, and the theory fits digital services best, not physical goods only.

#### Q11 · short_answer · applied · 4
A pet-sitting app paid $40 each to sign up its first 150 sitters. Ten months later it has 900 sitters: the other 750 joined without payment. Serving one more pet owner costs about $0.03, and 65% of owners open the app directly. Judge whether it behaves like an aggregator, using the theory’s components and the numbers.
**Sample:** It does. Direct relationship: 65% of owners start in the app instead of searching, so the app owns the demand. Near-zero marginal cost: $0.03 per extra owner means it scales without matching costs. Suppliers follow demand: all 750 sitters since then joined unpaid, so it no longer has to buy supply. The paid start with 150 sitters was a one-time push, not the ongoing model.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Uses the 65% direct opens as evidence of a direct user relationship
- 1 | Uses the $0.03 cost as evidence of near-zero marginal cost
- 1 | Uses the 750 unpaid sitters as evidence that supply follows demand
- 1 | Reaches a clear conclusion that it behaves like an aggregator

#### Q12 · short_answer · advanced · 4
Explain how an aggregator turns its suppliers into interchangeable options. Use an online marketplace for freelance translators as the example.
**Sample:** Clients start their search on the marketplace, so translators must be there to be found. As more clients arrive, more translators join, giving more choice, faster replies and better reviews, which attract still more clients. Inside the marketplace each translator appears as one card ranked by price, rating and speed, so the client compares cards, not brands. The translator loses the direct tie to the client, and the marketplace can take a growing commission that once went to agencies.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Explains the loop: more users attract more suppliers, which improves the experience and attracts more users
- 1 | Shows the commoditisation with a concrete detail (cards ranked by price, rating, speed)
- 1 | Shows the shift of power or profit to the marketplace (commission, lost client relationship)

#### Q13 · business_decision · advanced · 5
You run a chain of 12 driving schools. Half of your new students now come through a lesson-booking platform that charges 20%. The platform offers you a deal: commission falls to 12% if you list only with it. Choose one: accept the exclusive deal, list on two rival platforms as well, or keep the current listing and invest in direct channels (referral rewards, own booking page, school partnerships). Justify your choice with Aggregation Theory.
**Sample:** Keep the listing and invest in direct channels. The platform owns the relationship with half of my new students, and exclusivity would hand it even more power: once I depend on it fully, it can raise the 12% back up with little risk. Rival platforms spread risk, but each still sits between me and the student. Referral rewards and school partnerships build demand I own, and a past student who refers a friend costs less than 20% of a lesson package. Risk: direct channels grow slowly, so track the share of students coming direct each quarter.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Makes one clear choice
- 2 | Explains the power balance: the platform owns the user relationship and exclusivity deepens dependence
- 1 | Compares the options’ effect on who owns demand
- 1 | Names a risk and a metric to track

#### Q14 · business_decision · expert · 5
A fund will invest $3 million in one of two booking apps.
> App A (tickets for small music venues): 250,000 users, 80% of whom open the app directly; serving a user costs $0.02; after the first 50 venues were signed by hand, 400 more joined unpaid.
> App B (home nursing visits): 30,000 users; every booking needs a coordinator call costing $12; nurses still need $500 recruiting bonuses after two years.
Choose one. Judge each app on the aggregator components and name the main risk of your choice.
**Sample:** App A. It owns the user relationship (80% open it directly), serves users at $0.02 and has crossed the point where venues join without payment, so every new user makes the supply side stronger at no extra cost. App B has high marginal cost ($12 per booking) and still buys supply with $500 bonuses, so it scales like a staffing service, not an aggregator. The main risk for App A is that users are not locked in: a rival with a better experience could pull them away, so the money should go into the fan experience.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Judges both apps on direct relationship, marginal cost and supplier pull with numbers from the case
- 1 | Explains why App B’s $12 calls and $500 bonuses break aggregator dynamics
- 1 | Names a valid risk for the chosen app (no user lock-in, venues multi-homing, regulation, power without profit)
- 1 | Makes a clear choice that follows from the analysis

#### Q15 · case_study · expert · 6
> A family runs four guest houses in the mountains with yearly revenue of $900,000. Two booking sites bring 72% of all bookings and charge 17% commission. Guests who book direct return 30% of the time, but the booking sites mask guests’ email addresses, so the family has no contact list. Last year one site changed its ranking rules, and bookings fell 15% in a month.
Explain the family’s position using Aggregation Theory, calculate what it pays in commission each year, and propose a plan to reduce dependence with a measurable target.
**Sample:** The booking sites own the relationship with travellers, and the guest houses are interchangeable options in their listings, which is why a ranking change cut bookings 15%. Commission is 72% × $900,000 × 17% = $110,160 a year. Plan: collect guests’ contacts at check-in, offer direct bookers a free breakfast and 5% off (cheaper than 17%), and partner with local ski schools. Stay on both sites for new guests. Target: cut the sites’ share to 50% within two years, which at the same revenue cuts commission to $76,500, saving $33,660 before the perks.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Explains that the sites own the user relationship and the guest houses are commoditised, using the ranking shock as evidence
- 1 | Calculates $110,160 a year in commission correctly
- 2 | Proposes direct channels that fit the case (contacts at check-in, direct-booking perks, partnerships) while staying listed for discovery
- 1 | Sets a measurable target, such as a lower platform share with its commission effect

#### Q1 · single_choice · foundation · 1
До появи інтернету міська газета заробляла високу маржу на оголошеннях. Який ресурс, згідно з теорією агрегаторів, став дефіцитним натомість, коли дистрибуція стала майже безкоштовною?
A) Друкарські потужності
B) Увага користувачів
C) Фізичне місце на полицях
D) Запаси постачальників
**Answer:** B
**Why:** Коли витрати на дистрибуцію й трансакції падають майже до нуля, дефіцитом стає увага користувачів, і владу отримує той, хто володіє стосунками з ними. Друкарні й полиці були старою дефіцитною дистрибуцією, а запаси постачальників стають взаємозамінними, щойно агрегатор контролює попит.

#### Q2 · single_choice · foundation · 1
Який із цих бізнесів найкраще відповідає визначенню агрегатора?
A) Компанія таксі, що володіє 300 автомобілями й наймає всіх водіїв
B) Меблевий рітейлер, який продає через власні магазини
C) Кадрова агенція, яка особисто наймає кожну медсестру й платить бонуси за підписання
D) Застосунок рецептів із 2 млн користувачів, де фудблогери безкоштовно публікують рецепти, щоб до них дістатися, а кожен новий користувач коштує близько $0,01
**Answer:** D
**Why:** Застосунок рецептів володіє стосунками з користувачами, обслуговує нових майже безкоштовно й притягує постачальників, бо там аудиторія. Таксі й рітейлер володіють власною пропозицією, а агенція мусить залучати постачальників поодинці й за реальні гроші.

#### Q3 · true_false · foundation · 1
Агрегатор може домінувати на ринку, не володіючи товарами чи послугами, які на ньому продаються.
**Answer:** true
**Why:** Агрегатори контролюють попит, а не пропозицію. Постачальники приходять, бо там користувачі, тож агрегатор має владу, не володіючи готелями, автомобілями чи квартирами.

#### Q4 · true_false · applied · 1
Коли агрегатор має велику базу користувачів, вони прив’язані до нього, тож поліпшувати досвід користувача більше не потрібно.
**Answer:** false
**Why:** На боці попиту користувачів агрегатора майже ніщо не тримає, тож конкурент із кращим досвідом може їх переманити. Вважати владу агрегатора вічною це типова помилка.

#### Q5 · single_choice · applied · 1
Бутик-готель отримує 65% бронювань через платформу, що бере 18% комісії. Що теорія агрегаторів радить готелю як постачальнику?
A) Завтра ж піти з платформи й покладатися на власний сайт
B) Укласти з платформою ексклюзивну угоду заради вищого рейтингу
C) Виміряти свою залежність і будувати прямі канали, як-от базу email гостей і бонуси за пряме бронювання
D) Знизити ціни на платформі, щоб опинитися вище за сусідні готелі

**Why:** Теорія радить постачальникам виміряти, яка частка попиту йде через агрегатора, і будувати прямі канали. Різкий вихід кидає 65% бронювань, ексклюзивність поглиблює залежність, а знижки на платформі лише роблять готель дешевшим взаємозамінним варіантом.

#### Q10 · scenario_choice · advanced · 1
