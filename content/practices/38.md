## 38. Switching Costs & Lock-In / Витрати на перехід та прив’язка до постачальника

### EN

#### Q1 · single_choice · foundation · 1
A marketing agency is thinking about leaving its project tool. Which of these is a **procedural** switching cost?
A) A $600 penalty for ending the annual contract early
B) Losing the account manager who knows the agency’s quirks
C) Three weeks spent moving 400 projects and learning the new tool
D) Plug-ins bought for the old tool that will not work with the new one
**Answer:** C
**Why:** Procedural costs are the time spent searching, migrating data and learning a new system. The penalty is a financial cost, the account manager is a relational cost, and the plug-ins are durable purchases and complements.

#### Q2 · single_choice · foundation · 1
A coffee machine maker sells its machines below cost, and they only accept its own capsules. A household that wants another brand of capsules must buy a new machine. Which type of lock-in is this?
A) Network lock-in
B) Relational lock-in
C) Contractual lock-in
D) Durable purchases and complements
**Answer:** D
**Why:** The machine is a durable purchase that only works with one supplier’s complements, so leaving means throwing it away. Nobody else’s use of the machine matters (not network), there is no personal tie (not relational), and no contract is signed (not contractual).

#### Q3 · true_false · foundation · 1
Only costs that appear on an invoice, such as exit fees and setup charges, count as switching costs.
**Answer:** false
**Why:** Training time, lost data, broken habits and personal relationships are switching costs too, and they often weigh more than any fee. Counting only the price of leaving is one of the common mistakes.

#### Q4 · true_false · applied · 1
In a market with high switching costs, it is common to see deep discounts for new customers and higher prices later for the installed base.
**Answer:** true
**Why:** A customer won today will be costly to lose later, so firms bid hard at the start and then raise prices for customers who are already locked in.

#### Q5 · single_choice · applied · 1
A firm pays $9,000 a year for payroll software. A rival offers the same features for $7,000. Moving would cost a $3,000 migration fee, $2,500 of staff time for retraining and $1,500 for running both systems in parallel for a month. How long would the savings take to pay back the move?
A) 1.5 years
B) 2 years
C) 2.8 years
D) 3.5 years
**Answer:** D
**Why:** The full cost of leaving is $3,000 + $2,500 + $1,500 = $7,000, and the saving is $2,000 a year, so payback takes 3.5 years. 1.5 years counts only the migration fee and 2.8 years leaves out the parallel run, which is the mistake of counting only the visible price of leaving.

#### Q6 · multiple_select · applied · 2
A SaaS company for architecture studios wants to raise switching costs **through added value rather than penalties**. Which **two** moves fit?
A) Build integrations with the CAD tools studios already use
B) Introduce a three-year contract with a 50% early-exit fee
C) Store every project’s history and templates, so the tool grows more useful with use
D) Charge $2,000 to export a studio’s own data
E) Hide an automatic two-year renewal in the terms of service
**Answer:** A, C
**Why:** Integrations and accumulated history make the product more valuable the longer a studio stays, so leaving costs value, not a fine. The exit fee, export charge and hidden renewal are penalties that breed resentment and give rivals a “freedom to leave” message.

#### Q7 · scenario_choice · applied · 1
A new CRM costs 30% less than the market leader, yet after a year it has won almost none of the leader’s customers. Each customer would have to rebuild custom fields, move five years of deal history and retrain about 20 sales reps. What should the challenger do next?
A) Cut its price further, to 50% below the leader
B) Offer free data migration, on-site training and to buy out the remaining months of rivals’ contracts
C) Run ads saying the leader is overpriced
D) Wait until the leader’s customers grow unhappy and leave on their own
**Answer:** B
**Why:** The barrier is the cost of moving, not the price, so the challenger should absorb that cost for the buyer. Deeper price cuts and ads ignore switching costs, which is exactly the attacker’s common mistake, and waiting leaves the lock-in intact.

#### Q8 · scenario_choice · advanced · 2
A business internet provider ties clients to 36-month contracts with a $2,400 exit penalty. Its customer satisfaction score has fallen two years in a row. A fibre rival now advertises “no contract, leave any time” and offers to pay the penalty for anyone who switches. What is the best response?
A) Move from penalties to value: shorter contracts, better support and managed services such as network monitoring that clients would lose by leaving
B) Raise the exit penalty to $4,000 so the rival’s offer costs more
C) Give large discounts to new customers only and keep current prices for existing ones
D) Keep everything as it is, because the penalty still holds clients for now
**Answer:** A
**Why:** Lock-in built on penalties damages reputation and invites exactly this campaign, and the rival has already neutralised the penalty. A higher penalty deepens resentment, discounts only for newcomers anger the installed base, and standing still ignores that the barrier is being bought out.

#### Q9 · multiple_select · advanced · 2
An accounting firm raised its fees by 20% and kept 96% of its clients. The partners say this proves clients love the firm. Which **two** facts suggest the retention comes mainly from lock-in rather than satisfaction?
A) Clients’ eight years of tax records sit in the firm’s own system, which has no export function
B) The firm has more certified accountants than any rival in the city
C) In interviews, clients complain about the price but say changing accountants mid-year would mean repeating the audit
D) Referrals from existing clients rose by 30% this year
E) The firm won a regional award for client service
**Answer:** A, C
**Why:** Trapped data and the pain of repeating an audit are switching costs that hold clients regardless of how they feel, and the complaints show low satisfaction. Rising referrals and an award point to real satisfaction, and the number of accountants says nothing about why clients stay.

#### Q10 · scenario_choice · advanced · 1
A vendor of on-premise warehouse software has clients who each spent about $200,000 customising it. A cloud rival with built-in route optimisation appears, and early movers cut picking time by 25%. What does the theory of switching costs warn the vendor about?
A) Customisation protects it permanently, because nobody abandons a $200,000 investment
B) Regulators will soon force clients to stay with existing vendors
C) A new technology generation can make old investments worthless and erase the lock-in at once
D) Network lock-in will keep clients, because their suppliers use the same software
**Answer:** C
**Why:** In fast-changing markets a big enough gain lets clients write off sunk investments, so the barrier disappears. Customisation is not permanent protection, regulators tend to cut switching costs rather than raise them, and the case gives no sign of network lock-in.

#### Q11 · short_answer · applied · 4
A restaurant pays $250 a month for its till system. A rival offers the same for $180 a month. Moving means a $900 early-termination fee, 15 staff each spending 4 hours on training at $15 an hour, and $1,200 for new card terminals. Calculate the total measurable switching cost and the payback period, and name one switching cost the numbers leave out.
**Sample:** The saving is $70 a month, or $840 a year. Training costs 15 × 4 × $15 = $900, so the total is $900 + $900 + $1,200 = $3,000. Payback takes $3,000 / $840, about 3.6 years, so the move does not pay for a long time. The numbers leave out the loss of the customer loyalty data stored in the old system, plus the disruption of staff habits during busy service.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Calculates the yearly saving of $840
- 2 | Calculates the $3,000 total including $900 of training and a payback of about 3.6 years
- 1 | Names a hidden cost such as lost customer data, habits or relationships

#### Q12 · short_answer · advanced · 4
Explain why customers held mainly by exit penalties are a weak base for a business. Use a mobile phone operator as the example.
**Sample:** A penalty keeps customers without giving them a reason to want to stay. An operator with a 24-month contract and a high exit fee may show low churn, but its customers resent the trap and count the months until they can leave. When the contract ends, or when a rival offers to pay the fee or number portability makes moving easy, they leave in a wave. Meanwhile rivals advertise freedom to leave against it.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Explains the mechanism: penalties hold customers without value, so they resent it and leave at the first chance
- 1 | Applies it to a phone operator with a concrete detail (contract term, exit fee, porting the number)
- 1 | Names a trigger that releases them: contract end, rival buy-outs, regulation or a rival’s campaign

#### Q13 · business_decision · advanced · 5
You run project-management software for architecture studios at $40 per user per month. Monthly churn is 2%, and exit interviews say studios leave for cheaper tools. Choose one: introduce 24-month contracts with an exit fee of six months’ fees, build CAD integrations and a reusable project template library, or cut the price by 20%. Justify your choice using switching costs.
**Sample:** Build the integrations and template library. They raise switching costs through value: every project a studio runs adds templates and history it would lose by leaving, and the CAD link saves time daily. Exit fees would cut churn on paper but breed resentment and a reputation rivals can attack. A 20% cut gives away revenue on every studio, including the 98% who stay each month, and a cheaper tool can always undercut it. Risk: integrations take months, so track churn among studios that adopt them against those that do not.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Makes one clear choice
- 2 | Explains how the choice raises switching costs through value (history, templates, integrations)
- 1 | Explains why penalties or a price cut are weaker (resentment, reputation, lost revenue from stayers)
- 1 | Names a risk and a way to measure whether the choice works

#### Q14 · business_decision · expert · 5
You are a challenger in payroll software for firms with 50–200 employees.
> The leader charges $8 per employee per month; you charge $6. A typical client has 100 employees, so it would save $2,400 a year with you.
> Switching costs a client $4,000 for data migration, $1,500 of HR retraining time and $1,000 for a parallel payroll run.
> Option 1: cut your price to $5, so the client saves $3,600 a year.
> Option 2: keep $6, but do the migration and the parallel run yourself, at a one-off cost to you of $3,000 per client.
Choose one option. Show the client’s payback under each, and your cost.
**Sample:** Option 2. The client’s switching cost is $6,500. Under Option 1 it saves $3,600 a year, so payback is about 1.8 years, and I lose $1 × 100 × 12 = $1,200 a year per client for as long as it stays. Under Option 2 the client only bears $1,500 of retraining, so payback is $1,500 / $2,400, about 7.5 months, and my cost is a one-off $3,000, less than three years of the price cut. Absorbing the switching cost attacks the real barrier instead of competing on price alone.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Calculates both paybacks correctly (about 1.8 years and about 7.5 months)
- 1 | Compares the challenger’s cost: $1,200 a year forever against a one-off $3,000
- 1 | Explains that the barrier is the switching cost, not the price gap
- 1 | Makes a clear choice that follows from the numbers

#### Q15 · case_study · expert · 6
> A coffee roaster supplies 120 cafés. It lends each café an espresso machine worth $6,000 on condition that the café buys only its beans. Contracts run three years with a $3,000 exit fee. A barista trainer from the roaster visits every café each month. Last year the roaster raised bean prices by 18%; churn stayed at 3%, but cafés’ satisfaction score fell from 8.1 to 6.2 out of 10. A rival roaster now offers to pay any café’s exit fee and install its own machine for free.
Identify the switching costs that hold the cafés, judge whether low churn shows loyalty, and recommend how the roaster should respond.
**Sample:** Cafés are held by a contractual cost (three years, $3,000 fee), a durable complement (the loaned machine, which leaves with the contract) and a relational cost (the monthly trainer visits). Low churn does not prove loyalty: satisfaction fell from 8.1 to 6.2 after the price rise, so many cafés stay because leaving is costly. The rival’s offer removes both the fee and the machine barrier, so only the relationship remains. The roaster should rebuild value: roll back part of the 18% rise for cafés near renewal, expand the training into menu and staff support, and offer shorter contracts. It should watch satisfaction and renewal rates monthly, not churn alone.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Names at least three switching costs from the case (contract and fee, loaned machine, trainer relationship)
- 1 | Uses the drop from 8.1 to 6.2 to show retention comes from lock-in, not satisfaction
- 1 | Sees that the rival’s offer neutralises the fee and the machine, leaving only the relationship
- 2 | Recommends a value-based response (pricing, service, contract terms) and a metric beyond churn

#### Q1 · single_choice · foundation · 1
Маркетингова агенція думає відмовитися від свого інструмента для проєктів. Що з переліченого є **процедурною** витратою на перехід?
A) Штраф $600 за дострокове розірвання річного договору
B) Втрата менеджера, який знає всі особливості агенції
C) Три тижні на перенесення 400 проєктів і освоєння нового інструмента
D) Плагіни, куплені для старого інструмента, які не працюватимуть із новим
**Answer:** C
**Why:** Процедурні витрати це час на пошук, перенесення даних і освоєння нової системи. Штраф належить до фінансових витрат, менеджер до стосункових, а плагіни до тривалих покупок і доповнень.

#### Q2 · single_choice · foundation · 1
Виробник кавоварок продає їх нижче собівартості, і вони приймають лише його власні капсули. Родина, яка захоче капсули іншої марки, мусить купити нову кавоварку. Який це тип прив’язки?
A) Мережева прив’язка
B) Стосункова прив’язка
C) Договірна прив’язка
D) Тривалі покупки й доповнення
**Answer:** D
**Why:** Кавоварка є тривалою покупкою, що працює лише з доповненнями одного постачальника, тож перехід означає її викинути. Чужі користувачі тут нічого не важать (не мережева), особистого зв’язку немає (не стосункова), договору ніхто не підписував (не договірна).

#### Q3 · true_false · foundation · 1
Витратами на перехід вважаються лише ті, що з’являються в рахунку, як-от штрафи за вихід і платежі за підключення.
**Answer:** false
**Why:** Час на навчання, втрачені дані, зламані звички й особисті стосунки теж є витратами на перехід і часто важать більше за будь-який штраф. Рахувати лише ціну виходу це одна з типових помилок.

#### Q4 · true_false · applied · 1
На ринку з високими витратами на перехід часто бачимо великі знижки для нових клієнтів і вищі ціни згодом для наявної бази.
**Answer:** true
**Why:** Клієнта, здобутого сьогодні, згодом буде дорого втратити, тож компанії активно борються за нього на старті, а потім піднімають ціни для тих, хто вже прив’язаний.

#### Q5 · single_choice · applied · 1
Фірма платить $9 000 на рік за програму для нарахування зарплати. Конкурент пропонує ті самі функції за $7 000. Перехід коштуватиме $3 000 за перенесення даних, $2 500 робочого часу на перенавчання і $1 500 за місяць паралельної роботи обох систем. За який час економія окупить перехід?
A) 1,5 року
B) 2 роки
C) 2,8 року
D) 3,5 року
**Answer:** D
**Why:** Повна ціна виходу становить $3 000 + $2 500 + $1 500 = $7 000, а економія $2 000 на рік, тож окупність 3,5 року. 1,5 року враховує лише плату за перенесення, а 2,8 року не враховує паралельної роботи: це помилка, коли рахують лише видиму ціну виходу.

#### Q6 · multiple_select · applied · 2
SaaS-компанія для архітектурних бюро хоче підвищити витрати на перехід **через додану цінність, а не штрафи**. Які **два** кроки підходять?
A) Створити інтеграції з CAD-програмами, якими бюро вже користуються
B) Запровадити трирічний договір зі штрафом 50% за дострокове розірвання
C) Зберігати історію й шаблони кожного проєкту, щоб інструмент ставав кориснішим із часом
D) Брати $2 000 за вивантаження бюро власних даних
E) Сховати в умовах автоматичне продовження на два роки
**Answer:** A, C
**Why:** Інтеграції й накопичена історія роблять продукт ціннішим що довше бюро ним користується, тож вихід коштує втраченої цінності, а не штрафу. Штраф за вихід, плата за вивантаження і приховане продовження є покараннями, що викликають образу й дають конкурентам гасло «свободи виходу».

#### Q7 · scenario_choice · applied · 1

A) Ще знизити ціну, до 50% нижче за лідера
B) Запропонувати безкоштовне перенесення даних, навчання на місці й викуп решти місяців договору з конкурентом

**Why:** Бар’єр полягає у вартості переходу, а не в ціні, тож претендент має взяти цю вартість на себе. Ще більші знижки й реклама ігнорують витрати на перехід, і саме в цьому типова помилка атакувальника, а очікування лишає прив’язку недоторканою.

#### Q8 · scenario_choice · advanced · 2

#### Q10 · scenario_choice · advanced · 1
Постачальник локальної програми для складів має клієнтів, кожен з яких витратив близько $200 000 на її налаштування. #### Q11 · short_answer · applied · 4
Ресторан платить $250 на місяць за касову систему. Конкурент пропонує те саме за $180 на місяць. Перехід означає $900 штрафу за дострокове розірвання, навчання 15 працівників по 4 години при ставці $15 на годину і $1 200 за нові платіжні термінали. Обчисліть загальну вимірювану вартість переходу й строк окупності та назвіть одну витрату на перехід, якої немає в цифрах.
**Sample:** Економія становить $70 на місяць, або $840 на рік. Навчання коштує 15 × 4 × $15 = $900, тож загалом $900 + $900 + $1 200 = $3 000. Окупність $3 000 / $840, приблизно 3,6 року, тож перехід довго не окупиться. У цифрах немає втрати даних програми лояльності, що зберігаються в старій системі, а також збою звичок персоналу в години пік.
**Rubric:**
