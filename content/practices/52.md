## 52. Sales Funnel / Воронка продажів

### EN

#### Q1 · single_choice · foundation · 1
A funnel has three stages with conversion rates of 50%, 40% and 25%. What is the overall conversion from lead to customer?
A) 5%
B) 38%
C) 25%
D) 12.5%
**Answer:** A
**Why:** Stage rates multiply: 0.5 × 0.4 × 0.25 = 0.05, or 5%. 38% is the average of the rates, 25% is only the last stage, and 12.5% multiplies just two of the three stages.

#### Q2 · single_choice · foundation · 1
Which stage definition follows the rule of describing what the **buyer** did?
A) "Proposal sent"
B) "Lead called twice"
C) "Demo booked by the rep"
D) "Buyer agreed to an on-site visit"
**Answer:** D
**Why:** An exit rule based on a buyer action shows real progress and makes conversion rates comparable across the team. The other three describe what the salesperson did, which can happen whether or not the buyer moved forward.

#### Q3 · true_false · foundation · 1
Raising one weak stage's conversion rate from 20% to 25% has the same effect on final sales as getting 25% more leads.
**Answer:** true
**Why:** Because stage rates multiply, a rise from 20% to 25% lifts total output by a quarter, exactly like 25% more leads at the top. Fixing a stage is often much cheaper than buying the extra leads.

#### Q4 · true_false · applied · 1
A sales manager reports a $2 million pipeline by adding up all open deals, including several that have not been touched for nine months while the normal sales cycle is two months. This is a reliable forecast.
**Answer:** false
**Why:** Open deals should be weighted by their stage's historical win rate, and deals stuck far longer than the normal cycle should be removed. Counting everything at full value inflates the forecast.

#### Q5 · single_choice · applied · 1
An accounting firm wants 30 new clients next quarter. Its rates are: lead to qualified 50%, qualified to proposal 40%, proposal to signed 25%. How many leads does it need?
A) 120
B) 600
C) 300
D) 1,500
**Answer:** B
**Why:** Overall conversion is 0.5 × 0.4 × 0.25 = 5%, so 30 / 0.05 = 600 leads. 120 works back through the last stage only, 300 skips one stage, and 1,500 uses too low a rate.

#### Q6 · multiple_select · applied · 2
Pipeline velocity is opportunities × win rate × average deal size ÷ cycle length. Which **two** changes raise it, all else equal?
A) The sales cycle grows from 30 to 45 days
B) The win rate falls from 25% to 20%
C) The average deal size rises from $8,000 to $9,000
D) The number of open opportunities falls from 80 to 70
E) The sales cycle shrinks from 60 to 40 days
**Answer:** C, E
**Why:** A larger deal size raises the top of the formula, and a shorter cycle divides by a smaller number, so revenue moves through the funnel faster. A longer cycle, a lower win rate and fewer opportunities all lower velocity.

#### Q7 · scenario_choice · applied · 1
A marketing agency compares this quarter with past quarters:
> Leads: 400 (usually 400). Lead to meeting: 30% (usually 30%). Meeting to proposal: 60% (usually 60%). Proposal to signed: 10% (usually 25%).
The CEO wants to double the advertising budget. What should the agency do first?
A) Double the advertising budget, as planned
B) Hire another person to book more meetings
C) Interview prospects lost after the proposal and fix that stage
D) Cut prices on every service
**Answer:** C
**Why:** Only the proposal stage has dropped, from 25% to 10%, so the leak is there; learning why prospects leave shows what to fix. More leads or meetings would pour more prospects into the same leak, and a blanket price cut guesses at the cause.

#### Q8 · scenario_choice · advanced · 2
A software firm gets 800 leads a quarter. Lead to qualified is 40%, qualified to proposal 50%, proposal to won 20%. For the same $20,000 it can either buy 25% more leads or train the team so that proposal to won rises to 28%. Which gives more customers?
A) Buying leads: 25% more leads beats any single-stage fix
B) Training: about 45 customers against 40
C) Both are equal, since both raise output by 25%
D) Buying leads, because past stage rates cannot be changed
**Answer:** B
**Why:** Today: 800 × 0.4 × 0.5 × 0.2 = 32 customers. More leads: 1,000 × 0.4 × 0.5 × 0.2 = 40. Training: 160 proposals × 0.28 = about 45, because 20% to 28% is a 40% rise at that stage. Stage rates can be improved, and the two options are not equal.

#### Q9 · multiple_select · advanced · 2
A consultant warns a medical-equipment firm about relying too much on its sales funnel. Which **two** warnings describe genuine limitations of the model?
A) Hospital committees loop back and some buyers arrive already decided, so the path is not linear.
B) Stage rates multiply, so a weak stage hurts overall output.
C) Velocity divides by the length of the sales cycle.
D) Stage rates based on a few dozen deals can swing widely between quarters.
E) Stages are defined by what buyers do.
**Answer:** A, D
**Why:** Non-linear B2B buying and small samples are real weaknesses of the funnel. Multiplying rates, dividing by cycle length and buyer-based stages are features of the model, not limitations.

#### Q10 · scenario_choice · advanced · 1
A broadband provider started paying sales bonuses for conversion at every stage. Overall conversion rose from 8% to 12%, but the share of new customers cancelling within three months rose from 15% to 50%. What is the best reading?
A) The funnel change worked; cancellations are a separate issue
B) Spend more on leads to make use of the higher conversion
C) Shorten the sales cycle further to lock in the gain
D) Aggressive closing raised conversion but brought customers who leave, and the funnel ignores what happens after the sale
**Answer:** D
**Why:** Per 100 leads, customers still active after three months fell from 8 × 0.85 = 6.8 to 12 × 0.5 = 6. The funnel stops at the sale, so pushing conversion can reward closing customers who do not fit. More leads or a shorter cycle would scale the problem.

#### Q11 · short_answer · applied · 4
An online language school gets 2,000 trial sign-ups a quarter. 45% attend a first lesson, and 30% of those buy a $400 lesson package. Calculate customers, revenue and overall conversion. Then show the effect of raising trial-to-first-lesson to 55%.
**Sample:** Today 2,000 × 45% = 900 attend, and 900 × 30% = 270 buy, so revenue is 270 × $400 = $108,000 and overall conversion is 13.5%. At 55%, 1,100 attend and 330 buy, bringing $132,000: $24,000 more a quarter with no extra sign-ups.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Calculates 270 customers, $108,000 and 13.5% overall conversion
- 2 | Calculates 330 customers and $132,000 (or +$24,000) after the improvement

#### Q12 · short_answer · advanced · 4
Explain why funnel stages should be defined by what the buyer did rather than what the salesperson did. Give an example of a stage that misleads.
**Sample:** A stage such as "proposal sent" can be reached by a rep who emails a price to someone who never asked for it, so the deal looks advanced while the buyer has not moved. Different reps then count different things, and conversion rates cannot be compared or used for forecasts. A buyer-based rule such as "buyer requested a proposal after a site visit" shows real commitment, so stage rates mean the same for everyone and weighted pipeline values become reliable.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Explains that seller actions can happen without buyer progress, so stages do not show real movement
- 1 | Gives a concrete misleading seller-based stage and a buyer-based alternative
- 1 | Links the rule to comparable conversion rates or reliable forecasts

#### Q13 · business_decision · advanced · 5
A software firm has 120 open opportunities, a 25% win rate, a $10,000 average deal and a 60-day cycle. For the same cost it can: offer a 10% discount that lifts the win rate to 28%; add an implementation partner that cuts the cycle to 45 days; or run a campaign that raises opportunities to 140. Choose one using pipeline velocity.
**Sample:** Add the implementation partner. Today velocity is 120 × 0.25 × $10,000 ÷ 60 = $5,000 a day. The discount gives 120 × 0.28 × $9,000 ÷ 60 = $5,040, almost nothing, because the smaller deal eats the higher win rate. The campaign gives 140 × 0.25 × $10,000 ÷ 60 = about $5,833. The partner gives $300,000 ÷ 45 = about $6,667 a day, the largest gain. Risk: the 45-day cycle is an estimate, so I would check cycle length on the first 20 deals with the partner.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Makes one clear choice
- 2 | Calculates velocity for today and all three options ($5,000, $5,040, about $5,833, about $6,667)
- 1 | Explains why the discount barely helps (smaller deal offsets the higher win rate)
- 1 | Names a risk or how to verify the assumption

#### Q14 · business_decision · expert · 5
A B2B training company has two lead sources:
> Webinars: 500 leads a quarter for $30,000, overall conversion 4%, average deal $6,000.
> Partner referrals: 120 leads a quarter for $18,000 in referral fees, conversion 20%, average deal $9,000. Partners can supply at most 80 more leads at the same fee per lead.
It has an extra $30,000 a quarter. Choose: all into webinars, all into referrals, or a split. Justify with expected revenue.
**Sample:** Split: fill referrals to capacity, then put the rest into webinars. Referral leads cost $150 each and webinar leads $60. All into webinars buys 500 leads, 20 deals, $120,000. Referrals can take only 80 leads, $12,000, giving 16 deals and $144,000. The remaining $18,000 buys 300 webinar leads, 12 deals and $72,000, so the split brings $216,000. Risk: extra referrals may convert worse than the current 20% if partners send weaker contacts, so I would track their rate separately.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Makes one clear choice
- 2 | Calculates revenue of the webinar option ($120,000) and the referral part ($144,000 for 80 leads)
- 1 | Notices the 80-lead cap and uses the leftover budget, reaching about $216,000
- 1 | Names a risk such as falling conversion on the extra leads

#### Q15 · case_study · expert · 6
> An office fit-out company gets 400 leads a quarter. 50% qualify (200), 40% of those book a site survey (80), and 75% of surveys lead to a proposal (60).
> Only 15% of proposals are won, against 30% last year. The average project is $50,000.
> Notes on lost deals: 8 of 10 cite a six-week delivery time, while competitors deliver in three.
> Marketing asks for $40,000 to raise leads by 30%.
Calculate quarterly revenue now, with the marketing plan, and with the proposal stage back at 30%. Name the leak and recommend what to do.
**Sample:** Now: 60 proposals × 15% = 9 projects × $50,000 = $450,000. Marketing plan: 520 leads give 78 proposals and about 11.7 projects, about $585,000, a $135,000 gain. Restoring the proposal stage to 30%: 60 × 30% = 18 projects, $900,000, a $450,000 gain. The leak is the proposal stage, and the lost-deal notes point to delivery time, not price or leads. I would cut delivery to about three weeks, for example with stock of common items or a second installation crew, before buying more leads.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Calculates current revenue of $450,000
- 1 | Calculates the marketing plan at about $585,000
- 1 | Calculates the restored stage at $900,000
- 1 | Names the proposal stage as the leak and uses the lost-deal notes
- 2 | Recommends fixing delivery time with a concrete action and explains why it beats buying leads

#### Q3 · true_false · foundation · 1

**Why:** Конверсії етапів перемножуються, тож зростання з 20% до 25% збільшує підсумок на чверть, так само як 25% додаткових лідів на вході. Виправити етап часто значно дешевше, ніж купити ці ліди.

#### Q4 · true_false · applied · 1

#### Q13 · business_decision · advanced · 5
Софтверна фірма має 120 відкритих можливостей, частку виграшу 25%, середню угоду $10 000 і цикл 60 днів. #### Q15 · case_study · expert · 6
> Компанія з облаштування офісів отримує 400 лідів на квартал. 50% проходять кваліфікацію (200), 40% із них замовляють огляд приміщення (80), а 75% оглядів завершуються пропозицією (60).
> Виграють лише 15% пропозицій, тоді як торік вигравали 30%. Середній проєкт коштує $50 000.
> Нотатки про програні угоди: у 8 з 10 клієнти згадують термін виконання шість тижнів, тоді як конкуренти роблять за три.
> Маркетинг просить $40 000, щоб збільшити кількість лідів на 30%.
Порахуйте квартальний виторг зараз, із планом маркетингу та з поверненням конверсії пропозицій до 30%. Назвіть витік і порекомендуйте, що робити.
**Sample:** Зараз: 60 пропозицій × 15% = 9 проєктів × $50 000 = $450 000. План маркетингу: 520 лідів дають 78 пропозицій і близько 11,7 проєкту, тобто близько $585 000, приріст $135 000. Повернення етапу пропозиції до 30%: 60 × 30% = 18 проєктів, $900 000, приріст $450 000. Витік на етапі пропозиції, і нотатки про програні угоди вказують на термін виконання, а не на ціну чи кількість лідів. Я скоротив би термін приблизно до трьох тижнів, наприклад через запас популярних позицій або другу бригаду монтажу, перш ніж купувати ліди.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Рахує поточний виторг $450 000
- 1 | Рахує план маркетингу близько $585 000
- 1 | Рахує відновлений етап: $900 000
- 1 | Називає витоком етап пропозиції й спирається на нотатки про програні угоди
- 2 | Радить скоротити термін виконання конкретною дією й пояснює, чому це краще за купівлю лідів
